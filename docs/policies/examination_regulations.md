# Academic Regulations — Part V: Examination and Evaluation

**School of Technology · Office of the Controller of Examinations**
**Document ref.:** AR/V/2026 · **Effective from:** Academic Year 2026-27 (Odd Semester, 16 June 2026) · **Supersedes:** AR/V/2024

## 1. Scope

1.1 This Part sets out the scheme of continuous internal assessment and end-semester examination for undergraduate courses, the criteria for passing, the treatment of absence and backlogs, the computation of grade points, the declaration of results, and the consequences of examination malpractice.

1.2 It applies to all course offerings in all departments of the School of Technology: Computer Science, Information & Communication Technology, Electronics & Communication, Mechanical, Civil, Chemical, and Basic Sciences & Humanities.

1.3 Admission to an end-semester examination is additionally subject to the attendance requirement in Part IV (Attendance) and to clearance of dues under the Fee Policy (FP/2026).

## 2. Scheme of assessment

2.1 Every course is assessed through **continuous internal assessment (CIA)** conducted during the semester and an **end-semester examination (ESE)** conducted after the last teaching day.

2.2 **Theory courses.** The assessment components, their maximum marks and their weightage towards the course total are:

| Component | Title | Max. marks | Weightage | Indicative week |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quiz-1 | Quiz 1 | 10 | 5 % | Week 3 |
| Assignment-1 | Assignment 1 | 10 | 7 % | Week 4 |
| Internal-1 | Internal Test 1 | 20 | 15 % | Week 6 |
| Assignment-2 | Assignment 2 | 10 | 8 % | Week 9 |
| Internal-2 | Internal Test 2 | 20 | 15 % | Week 12 |
| End-Sem | End Semester Examination | 100 | 40 % | Examination period |

2.2.1 The five CIA components together carry 50 per cent of the course total and the ESE carries 40 per cent; the remaining 10 per cent is awarded by the course instructor for tutorial work, class participation and problem-solving, recorded as *Term-Work*.

2.2.2 Internal tests are conducted university-wide in the windows published in the Academic Calendar. For the Odd Semester 2026-27, Internal Test 1 is held from 20 to 25 August 2026 and Internal Test 2 from 21 to 26 September 2026.

2.3 **Laboratory courses.** Laboratory courses are assessed through *Lab-CIE* (continuous evaluation of each experiment, 40 per cent), *Term-Work* (laboratory record and viva, 20 per cent) and a *Lab-Exam* at the end of the semester (40 per cent).

2.4 **Assignments** are submitted through the student portal by the due date set by the instructor. A submission after the due date but within three calendar days is recorded as *late* and attracts a deduction of 25 per cent of the marks awarded. A submission more than three days late, or no submission, is recorded as *missing* and scores zero. Late submission does not affect a submission's eligibility for the marks otherwise awarded.

2.5 The course instructor enters marks for each component in the University examination system within seven working days of the assessment. Marks are visible to students on the portal once entered. A student may query a CIA mark with the instructor within five working days of its publication.

## 3. Criteria for passing

3.1 **The minimum mark for passing on any assessment component is 40 (forty) per cent of the maximum marks of that component**, and the minimum for passing a course is 40 per cent of the course total after weightage.

3.2 A student who scores below 40 per cent in the end-semester examination fails the course irrespective of CIA marks, and a student who scores below 40 per cent in aggregate CIA fails the course irrespective of the ESE mark.

3.3 A student who fails a course is recorded as having a **backlog** in that course and may clear it under clause 5.

3.4 For a laboratory course the student must additionally have completed and had certified at least 80 per cent of the prescribed experiments to be eligible for the Lab-Exam.

## 4. Absence from an assessment

4.1 A student who is absent from a CIA component is awarded zero marks for that component and is recorded as *absent* in the examination system.

4.2 A make-up assessment for a missed internal test may be granted by the Head of Department, on an application submitted **within five working days** of the test, on medical grounds supported by a certificate meeting the standard in Part IV clause 5.3.1, or where the student was representing the University under Part IV clause 5.3.2. One make-up is permitted per component per semester. Quizzes and assignments are not made up; the instructor may at their discretion set an alternative task of equivalent weight.

4.3 A student who is absent from an end-semester examination, or from a laboratory examination, is awarded zero in that examination and fails the course, and must clear the backlog under clause 5, except that a student prevented from appearing by hospitalisation certified under Part IV clause 5.3.1 may be permitted by the Controller of Examinations to appear in the next scheduled supplementary examination as a first attempt.

## 5. Backlogs, supplementary and re-examination

5.1 A student with a backlog in a course clears it by passing the end-semester examination of that course at a subsequent scheduled examination — a **supplementary examination** held within four weeks of the declaration of results, or the regular examination of the semester in which the course is next offered. CIA marks of the original registration are carried forward for two academic years for this purpose, provided the student had passed CIA in aggregate.

5.2 A student who failed CIA in aggregate, or was detained under Part IV clause 4.5, must re-register for the course, attend it afresh and complete all assessment components again.

5.3 The maximum grade awarded on clearing a backlog by supplementary examination is *B*. A course cleared by re-registration is graded without this limit.

5.4 A student may not carry more than **six backlogs** at the start of any semester; a student exceeding this limit is required to reduce the backlogs to six or fewer before registering for further courses. A student who fails to complete the programme within twice its normal duration (eight years for a four-year programme) is removed from the rolls.

## 6. Grading and grade points

6.1 Marks for a course are converted to a letter grade and grade point on a ten-point scale as follows:

| Course total (%) | Grade | Grade point |
|---|---|---|
| 90 and above | O | 10 |
| 80 – 89 | A+ | 9 |
| 70 – 79 | A | 8 |
| 60 – 69 | B+ | 7 |
| 50 – 59 | B | 6 |
| 45 – 49 | C | 5 |
| 40 – 44 | P | 4 |
| Below 40, absent, or detained | F | 0 |

6.2 The **Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA)** is the credit-weighted average of grade points over all courses registered in the semester. The **Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA)** is the credit-weighted average over all semesters completed. Both are reported to two decimal places; grade points for a failed course are counted as zero until the backlog is cleared, after which the new grade point replaces it.

6.3 Credits are earned only for courses passed. The result for a semester is recorded as *Pass* if every course in that semester has been passed, and *Fail* otherwise.

6.4 Classification of the degree on completion: CGPA 8.50 and above, *First Class with Distinction*; 7.00 to 8.49, *First Class*; 6.00 to 6.99, *Second Class*; 5.00 to 5.99, *Pass Class*.

## 7. Examination form, hall ticket and conduct

7.1 Every student must submit the end-semester examination form through the student portal during the window published in the Academic Calendar. For the Odd Semester 2026-27 the window is **12 to 20 October 2026**. A late submission is accepted up to five days after the window with a late fee of ₹500; no form is accepted after that.

7.2 A hall ticket is issued only to a student who (a) has submitted the examination form, (b) is eligible in the course under Part IV, and (c) has no outstanding dues, or holds an instalment approval, under the Fee Policy. Hall tickets are issued on the portal at least seven days before the first examination.

7.3 End-semester examinations for the Odd Semester 2026-27 are conducted from **17 to 28 November 2026**, after the Diwali vacation (7 to 15 November 2026). The detailed timetable is published on the portal at least three weeks before the first examination.

7.4 Students must carry the hall ticket and the University identity card to every examination, arrive at least fifteen minutes before the start, and may not enter more than thirty minutes after the start or leave within the first hour. Mobile telephones, smart watches and any material not expressly permitted on the question paper are prohibited in the examination hall.

## 8. Declaration of results, revaluation and transcripts

8.1 Results are declared on the student portal on the date published in the Academic Calendar — **18 December 2026** for the Odd Semester 2026-27 and 9 June 2026 for the preceding Even Semester.

8.2 A student may apply for **revaluation** of an end-semester answer script within **fifteen calendar days** of the declaration of results, on payment of ₹500 per course, refundable if the mark changes by five per cent or more of the maximum. Revaluation is by a second examiner; the higher of the two marks stands if they differ by up to ten per cent of the maximum, otherwise a third examiner's mark is final. Internal assessment marks are not subject to revaluation.

8.3 Semester grade cards are issued on the portal within one week of the declaration of results. Official transcripts are requested under the Student Services Policy (SS/2026).

## 9. Examination malpractice

9.1 Malpractice includes possession of unauthorised material or devices, communication with another candidate, copying, impersonation, tampering with an answer book or hall ticket, and any attempt to influence an examiner. Proxy attendance in a course is dealt with under Part IV clause 3.6.

9.2 An invigilator who observes malpractice records it on the prescribed form and the matter is referred to the Examination Malpractice Committee, which hears the student within ten working days.

9.3 Penalties are graded as follows:

9.3.1 Possession of unauthorised material not shown to have been used — cancellation of the paper concerned.

9.3.2 Use of unauthorised material, copying or communication — cancellation of all papers of that examination; the student appears afresh at the next scheduled examination.

9.3.3 Impersonation, tampering with an answer book, or a second offence — cancellation of all papers of that examination and debarment from the University for one semester.

9.3.4 Any attempt to influence an examiner — debarment for one academic year, and referral to the Discipline Committee under Part VII.

9.4 A student may appeal a decision of the Examination Malpractice Committee to the Vice-Chancellor within seven days; the appeal decision is final.

## 10. Review

10.1 The Academic Council reviews this Part every two years. Questions of interpretation are referred to the Controller of Examinations, whose ruling applies until the Council next meets.
