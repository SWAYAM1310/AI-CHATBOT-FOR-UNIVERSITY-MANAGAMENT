# Academic Regulations — Part VII: Student Code of Conduct, Discipline and Anti-Ragging

**School of Technology · Office of the Dean of Student Affairs**
**Document ref.:** AR/VII/2026 · **Effective from:** Academic Year 2026-27 · **Supersedes:** AR/VII/2024

## 1. Purpose and scope

1.1 This Part states the standard of conduct expected of every student of the School of Technology on campus, in hostels, during University activities off campus, and online in connection with the University; prohibits ragging absolutely; establishes the bodies that deal with complaints and misconduct; and sets out the penalties that may be imposed.

1.2 It applies to all students from the date of admission until the date the degree is conferred, and to former students in respect of conduct during their studies.

1.3 The University's regulations on ragging give effect to the UGC Regulations on Curbing the Menace of Ragging in Higher Educational Institutions, 2009, as amended, and to the directions of the Supreme Court of India. Where this Part is silent, those regulations apply.

## 2. General standard of conduct

2.1 Students are expected to be honest in academic work, respectful to fellow students, staff and visitors, and careful of University property and reputation.

2.2 Every student carries the University identity card on campus and produces it when asked by a member of staff or security. Access to laboratories, the library, hostels and examination halls requires the card.

2.3 Attendance, examinations and academic integrity are governed by Parts IV and V. Proxy attendance (Part IV clause 3.6) and examination malpractice (Part V clause 9) are also misconduct under this Part where the Committees under those Parts refer them.

2.4 Plagiarism — presenting another person's work, or text generated by a tool, as one's own in an assignment, report or project — is academic misconduct. A first instance in an assignment scores zero for that assignment; a repeat, or plagiarism in a project report, is referred to the Discipline Committee.

## 3. Prohibited conduct

3.1 The following are misconduct under this Part:

3.1.1 ragging, as defined in clause 4;

3.1.2 violence, threats, harassment (including sexual harassment, which is dealt with by the Internal Complaints Committee under clause 7.3), and discrimination on any ground;

3.1.3 possession or consumption of alcohol, narcotic or psychotropic substances, or tobacco products on campus or in hostels, or attending University activities under their influence;

3.1.4 damage to, theft of, or unauthorised use of University property, including computing and network resources;

3.1.5 forging or altering any University document, or making a false statement to obtain a benefit;

3.1.6 conduct on social media or online that harasses a member of the University community or brings the University into disrepute;

3.1.7 unauthorised entry into a hostel of the opposite sex, or presence in a hostel after the notified hours without permission of the Warden;

3.1.8 organising or taking part in a protest or gathering that obstructs teaching, examinations or access to buildings, without the permission of the Dean of Student Affairs;

3.1.9 breach of a laboratory safety instruction or of the terms of a signed laboratory undertaking; and

3.1.10 failure to comply with a lawful instruction of a member of staff, or with a penalty imposed under this Part.

## 4. Ragging

4.1 **Definition.** Ragging means any conduct, by words spoken or written or by an act, which has the effect of teasing, treating or handling with rudeness a fresher or any other student; indulging in rowdy or indisciplined activities which cause or are likely to cause annoyance, hardship, physical or psychological harm, or raise fear or apprehension in a fresher or any other student; asking a student to do any act which the student will not in the ordinary course do and which causes shame, torment or embarrassment; any act by a senior student that prevents, disrupts or disturbs the regular academic activity of any other student; exploiting the services of a fresher or other student for completing academic tasks; any act of financial extortion or forceful expenditure; any act of physical abuse; any act or abuse by spoken words, e-mails, posts, public insults, or the like; and any act that affects the mental health and self-confidence of a fresher or other student, whether with or without the intent to derive a sadistic pleasure or show off power, authority or superiority.

4.2 **Ragging in any form is absolutely prohibited**, inside and outside the campus, in hostels, in transport, and online. It is punishable under this Part, and may also be an offence under the Indian Penal Code and the Gujarat Prohibition of Ragging Act.

4.3 **Undertakings.** Every student, and a parent or guardian of the student, submits at admission and at the start of each academic year the anti-ragging undertaking in the form prescribed by the UGC, on the national anti-ragging portal and on the student portal. A student who has not submitted the undertaking is not registered for the semester.

4.4 **Anti-Ragging Committee and Squad.** The Anti-Ragging Committee, chaired by the Vice-Chancellor's nominee and including faculty, non-teaching staff, parents, students and a representative of the local police and civil administration, oversees the University's measures against ragging and decides complaints. The Anti-Ragging Squad, comprising faculty and wardens, makes unannounced visits to hostels and other places where ragging may occur, particularly during the first six weeks of the Odd Semester (16 June to 31 July 2026), and investigates complaints on the spot.

4.5 **Reporting.** A student who is ragged, or who witnesses ragging, may report it by any of the following, all of which are available round the clock:

4.5.1 the University anti-ragging helpline (toll free), the number of which is printed on the identity card and displayed in every hostel and building;

4.5.2 the online complaint form on the student portal, which may be submitted anonymously;

4.5.3 the national anti-ragging helpline 1800-180-5522 or helpline@antiragging.in;

4.5.4 any member of the Anti-Ragging Squad, any Warden, the Dean of Student Affairs, or any faculty member, who must forward the report to the Committee the same day.

4.6 **All complaints are handled confidentially.** The identity of a complainant or witness is not disclosed to the person complained of without the complainant's consent, and retaliation against a complainant or witness is itself misconduct under clause 3.1.

4.7 **Procedure.** On receipt of a complaint the Anti-Ragging Squad records it, ensures the safety and medical care of the affected student, and reports to the Committee within twenty-four hours. The Committee hears the student complained of within seven days and decides the matter within fifteen days of the complaint. Where the conduct may amount to a criminal offence, the University also lodges a first information report with the police.

4.8 **Penalties for ragging** are one or more of: suspension from classes and academic privileges; withholding of scholarships and other benefits; debarment from examinations; withholding of results; debarment from representing the University in any event; suspension or expulsion from the hostel; cancellation of admission; rustication for one to four semesters; expulsion from the University with a bar on admission to any other institution for a stated period; and a fine of up to ₹2,50,000. Where the persons who ragged cannot be identified, the Committee may impose a collective penalty on the students of the hostel wing or class concerned.

4.9 A student who is found to have made a deliberately false complaint of ragging is dealt with under clause 3.1.5.

## 5. Hostel rules in brief

5.1 Hostel residents comply with the Hostel Rules issued by the Chief Warden, which form part of this Code. In particular: residents are in the hostel by 22:30 on all days unless on leave recorded with the Warden; visitors are received only in the visitors' area during 09:00 to 19:00; cooking appliances and inflammable material are not kept in rooms; residents are responsible for the condition of the room allotted to them and are charged for damage; and the Warden or a member of the Anti-Ragging Squad may enter any room at any time.

5.2 A resident who wishes to be absent overnight records the absence with the Warden in advance through the portal. This is separate from academic leave under the Student Leave Policy (SL/2026).

5.3 Breach of the Hostel Rules is dealt with by the Chief Warden, who may impose a warning, a fine of up to ₹2,000, or removal from the hostel, and may refer serious or repeated breaches to the Discipline Committee.

## 6. Dress and identity

6.1 Students dress in a manner appropriate to a professional educational institution. Departments prescribe safety clothing and footwear for laboratories and workshops, and a student not so dressed is not admitted to the session and is marked absent.

6.2 The University identity card is worn visibly on campus. A lost card is reported the same day on the portal and replaced on payment of ₹200 under the Fee Policy clause 2.3.

## 7. Committees and grievance redressal

7.1 **Discipline Committee.** Chaired by the Dean of Student Affairs, with three faculty members, one Warden and two student members nominated by the Students' Council, the Committee hears all matters of misconduct under clause 3 other than ragging and those reserved to other bodies. It gives the student written notice of the allegation, hears the student in person, allows the student to be accompanied by a fellow student or faculty member, and decides within twenty working days of the reference.

7.2 **Anti-Ragging Committee.** Constituted under clause 4.4; hears ragging complaints.

7.3 **Internal Complaints Committee.** Constituted under the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013, and the UGC regulations of 2015; receives and decides complaints of sexual harassment, with its own procedure and timelines, which take precedence over clause 7.1.

7.4 **Student Grievance Redressal Committee.** Receives any grievance of a student concerning academic or administrative matters — including a decision under Parts IV and V, a fee dispute not resolved under FP/2026 clause 8, or a delay under SS/2026 — that has not been resolved by the office concerned. A grievance is lodged on the portal; the Committee acknowledges it within three working days and decides it within fifteen working days. A student dissatisfied with the decision may appeal to the Ombudsperson appointed under the UGC (Redressal of Grievances of Students) Regulations, 2023.

## 8. Penalties

8.1 The Discipline Committee may impose, singly or in combination, in ascending order of gravity: a written warning entered in the student's record; a fine of up to ₹10,000 or the cost of damage, whichever is greater; community service on campus of up to forty hours; withdrawal of specific privileges (hostel, library, sports, representation of the University) for a stated period; suspension from the University for up to one semester; rustication for up to four semesters; and expulsion.

8.2 A penalty of suspension or above is entered on the student's character certificate under SS/2026 clause 3.8, and a scholarship held by the student is suspended under SF/2026 clause 7.2.

8.3 A student may appeal a decision of the Discipline Committee to the Vice-Chancellor within ten days. The Vice-Chancellor may confirm, reduce or set aside the penalty; the decision on appeal is final.

8.4 A penalty takes effect on the expiry of the appeal period or on the decision on appeal, except that a suspension imposed for the safety of others takes effect immediately.

## 9. Review

9.1 The Students' Welfare Committee reviews this Part every two years, and the Anti-Ragging Committee reviews clause 4 annually in the light of any change in the UGC regulations.
